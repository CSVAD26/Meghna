## Assignment 2

In this sketch, I wanted to create three movable swatches (extedning the PaletteExplorer sketch) with toggleable triadic and adjacent palettes. The swatches can be clicked and dragged to new positions for the user to see if the swatches match their design preferences based on triadic and adjacent palettes. You can toggle between the 2 palettes and toggle on and off the brightness gradient strips. When changing the color of one of the swatches, the other swatches update to maintain the selected palette relationship. The sketch is designed to help explore color combinations and arrangements that suit your preferences.

## Assignment 3

This sketch extends the OpenCV K-means Color example by processing live webcam video instead of a user-uploaded image. It groups the pixels in each frame into color clusters and displays a simplified version of the video using those representative colors. The current number of clusters is shown on screen. You can press + or = to increase the number of clusters, or − to decrease it (to a minimum of two). Changing the cluster count lets you explore how it affects the amount of colors and details in the video.

## Assignment 4
This sketch visualizes cycling workouts as concentric oval tracks. The track size represents distance, the color represents calories burned, and each workout has a moving dot whose speed is based on distance divided by duration. You can hover over a track to see the workout’s date, distance, energy, and duration. Press Space to play or pause the animation, or click Redo to reset it while paused.

## Project 1
In this project, I created a 2D frame-by-frame animation tool called *Fun Animation Lab*. Designed to remove the steep learning curve of traditional animation software, the tool lets you sketch, color, and animate without needing prior technical knowledge. It introduces an aesthetic constraint where your palette dynamically interpolates between a start and end hue across frames, linking color progression directly with motion. It features onion-skinning, flexible frame counts (2–9 frames), adjustable frame rates, and loop or ping-pong playback modes. For a full reflection on the design process, UX insights, and review learnings, see the complete [Project 1 Readme](./Projects/Project1/README.md).

## Project 2
*My Films & Books Log* is an interactive visualization comparing my Letterboxd film ratings and StoryGraph book ratings with public ratings. Movie posters and book covers are plotted by my rating and the public rating, with predicted ratings shown for my watchlist and to-be-read books. You can explore six views, filter by genres and reading themes, zoom and pan around the plot, and enter ratings to update the predictions. For more details, see the complete [Project 2 README](./Projects/Project2/README.md).
